15 · SDRAM stream fabric: architecture specification

The 64 MB SDRAM holds the parameters of a small transformer (example 14 puts them there). This example moves them out again as fast as the chip allows and hands them to N compute units through N FIFOs, each unit owning its own region of memory, with one write-back channel for results. The compute units of this example do the one operation a transformer spends its time on: an int8 matrix-vector product. Everything else about the LLM comes later; this page is about the data flowing.

Specification for review: nothing built yet Target: stories15M (15.2 MB int8); bring-up: stories260K Quartus Prime Lite 17.1 No shield needed
How to read this page. Each section ends with the decision it asks you to confirm. The numbers marked expected are calculated from the data sheet and from example 14's measurements; nothing on this page has run yet. Section 17 lists the open questions in one place.

3 · The SDRAM side: sdram_burst_ctrl

Why bursts

The SDRAM's data bus is 16 bits wide and moves one word per clock: at 100 MHz that is 200 MB/s, the ceiling. A single-word access pays the row open/close every time (example 06's controller: about 13 clocks per read at 50 MHz, ≈ 8 MB/s). A burst pays it once per L words: ACT, one wait clock (tRCD), L column commands, the precharge and its tRP. Measured in simulation (lib/sim/tb_sdram_burst_ctrl.sv, 9 bursts back to back, refresh included):

Decided: CL 3 and the BL = 1 "controller makes the burst" approach (versus the chip's own burst modes, which would need interrupting bursts and give nothing here); explicit PRE rather than auto-precharge (above). Verified in simulation against the JEDEC-timed model: 550 bursts with every length and every boundary, 0 data errors, 0 timing errors, also with a refresh forced every 97 clocks (a pause inside almost every burst).

4 · The fabric: stream_fabric

The two rules that keep the bus busy

  1. Credit. A read channel asks for a burst only when its FIFO has room for the whole burst (depth − level − words already in flight ≥ BURST). A write lane asks only when it holds the whole burst (or is flushing). So a burst, once granted, never stalls in the middle: the SDRAM port needs no backpressure and every granted clock moves a word.
  2. One burst per grant. The arbiter is round-robin over the 2N streaming requesters (N read channels, N write lanes) at burst granularity; the legacy bridge is not in the ring, it is served first (below). Fairness costs nothing: with credits, a channel that has room is a channel whose unit is consuming.

Continuity needs FIFO depth ≥ 2 × BURST: while the unit drains one burst the next one is being fetched. The demo uses DEPTH 1024 words (two M9K in 1024 × 9 mode… see section 9) and BURST ≤ 512. An "emptiest first" policy (grant the channel with the lowest level) was considered and not built: plain round-robin already measures 96.6 % of the bus with every channel finishing within one burst of the others (section 11), so there was nothing left to gain.

Who owns the read data. The controller accepts the next command (cmd_ready) while the previous read burst's last words are still on their way (section 3: the data trails the READ by rd_delay + 1 clocks, the PRE and tRP run meanwhile). So the arbiter keeps a 4-entry queue of accepted commands (kind and owner: read channel, write lane, legacy read or write): every accepted command pushes, every done pops (the controller finishes commands strictly in order), and rvalid words go to the owner at the head. Two read bursts' data never overlap (there are at least 4 clocks between the last word of one and the first of the next), so the head is always unambiguous. The owner of wdata is set when a write is accepted and cleared when its done pops it; at most three commands are ever outstanding, and a fault flag (STATUS bit 5) catches the queue overflowing, which must never happen.

Write-back lane (wb_lane)

A unit pushes 32-bit results (wb_data, wb_valid, wb_ready); the lane stores them in a 64 × 32 FIFO and writes them as 16-bit words, low half first, to WB_BASE, which auto-increments. It requests a burst when it holds WBURST words (default 64 words = 32 results) or when the unit raises wb_flush at the end of a job and anything is left. Registers: WB_BASE (25), WB_CTRL ([10:0] WBURST, bit 16 flush self-clearing, bit 17 clear), WB_STAT ([6:0] level in results, bit 7 underrun: the lane was granted a burst and ran out of results, it sent zeros and the pointer stayed consistent; sticky until clear, [31:8] words written).

Legacy bridge

The example 06/14 master port (req, we, addr[24:0], wdata, ready, rvalid, rdata) becomes a burst of one word that is served first: the word goes straight to the controller ahead of the arbiter's slot, so its wait is bounded by the one burst in progress plus the refreshes that came due during it, ≈ 1024 + 5 + 2 × 12 clocks ≈ 10.5 µs at BURST 1024 (2.8 µs at 256). Measured in tb_stream_fabric: a worst wait of 1063 clocks with 1024-word bursts in flight. The loader's words arrive every 3.4 µs at the cable's rate and its 16-word FIFO absorbs that; in practice the loader runs when no job runs. This is how example 14 stays untouched and still proven (R5).

async_fifo

Own module instead of Altera's dcfifo so Icarus simulates it: binary pointers, gray-coded copies crossed with 2-FF synchronizers, inferred simple dual-port RAM (one M9K per 1024 × 9 or 512 × 18), show-ahead read, level on both sides from the synchronized pointers (conservative on each side, which is exactly what the credit rule needs). With both clocks tied it is an ordinary synchronous FIFO two clocks of latency slower, which the demo accepts for the sake of R7.

Decision asked: credit-based bursts with round-robin, DEPTH 1024 / BURST 256 as defaults, one 64-entry lane per unit.

5 · The PC side: jtag_regs, fabric_ctl.py, the register map

jtag_regs is a second virtual JTAG node (sld_virtual_jtag, instance index 1; the loader is 0) with a 4-bit IR: 1 ADDR (shift 16 bits: the register address), 2 WRITE (shift 32 bits; on update the word is written to ADDR and ADDR increments), 3 READ (capture returns the register at ADDR; on update ADDR increments), 4 ID (capture a fixed 32-bit identifier and version). Auto-increment makes a block transfer one DR pass per word. The TCK ↔ clk crossing reuses the loader's pattern (toggle + 2-FF synchronizer + acknowledge). On the fabric side it is a plain bus: addr[15:0], wdata[31:0], we, rdata[31:0], one clock per access.

scripts/fabric_ctl.py drives it over both routes of example 14 (usb_blaster.py's SldHub directly, or quartus_stp with a small Tcl answer file): regs (dump), poke / peek, calib (sweep rd_delay with a known pattern written by the loader), memtest (full chip through the fabric at a chosen BURST), bench (loop mode on all channels, report MB/s and utilisation), job (program a matvec job from the model's JSON map, run it, wait for done), stats.

Weight stream format (model.q8.bin)

Each matrix W (ROWS × K) is stored row after row. A row is split along K into groups of G = 32 weights (32 divides 288 and 768; stories260K's 172 is padded to 192 with zeros). A group on the stream is 17 words: 16 words of int8 pairs (word j = {w[2j+1], w[2j]}, little-endian) followed by one word holding the group's int16 scale s_g. The real weight is w = q · s_g · 2E, with E one exponent per matrix kept in the JSON map; the exporter picks E so that the largest s_g is 32767. The hardware never sees E or the activation scale: it computes Σg (Σj qj xj) · sg exactly and the host applies the two scalars. Overhead of the scales: 1/16 = 6 %. Every matrix starts on a 1024-word boundary (a row of the SDRAM) so no burst of a power-of-two length ever crosses a row inside a matrix.

  g0: w[0..31] as 16 words | s_0 | g1: w[32..63] as 16 words | s_1 | ... | g8 | s_8
row i of W, K = 768: 24 groups × 17 words = 408 words
matrix: ROWS × (K/32 × 17) words, then zero padding to the next multiple of 1024 words

7 · The compute: matvec_unit

A unit computes y = Wslice · x for its slice of rows (ROWS of them, K wide) against T activation vectors at once (T = 1 for decoding, up to 8 for prefill or batching), which is where the array pays off: every weight word read from memory is used 2 · T times. The weights stream; the activations stay.

Per clock (as built in lib/rtl/matvec_unit.sv)

pop one word from the FIFO  →  two int8 weights w0 (column k), w1 (column k+1)
for every t in 0..T_MAX-1 in parallel:
    read x_t[k..k+3] from x-buffer t           (one M9K as 256 × 32: one word per clock, the pair
                                                selected by the word's parity)
    p_t = w0 · x_t[k] + w1 · x_t[k+1]          (two 9 × 9 signed multiplies, 17-bit result)
    gsum_t += p_t                              (21-bit signed: |16 · 2 · 128 · 128| = 2^19 fits)
every 17th word is the scale s_g:
    ghold ← gsum, gsum ← 0 (so the next group starts at once), then one t per clock:
    acc_t += ghold_t × s_g                     (21 × 16 → 37 bits into a 48-bit accumulator;
                                                T + 2 clocks, always under the 17 pops of a group)
end of a row (K/32 groups):
    swap the accumulator bank; for every t in use: y = saturate_int32(acc_t >>> SHIFT) →
    write-back lane (wb_data), acc_t = 0: 3 clocks per value, overlapped with the next row's
    pops; the unit holds the next row's LAST scale word until the previous row is fully written
    (that wait is CYCLES_STALLED)
end of the job (ROWS rows): wb_flush, STAT.done

Three pipeline stages (word register, products, group sum) and a 3-stage MAC: a weight word is in the group sum 4 clocks after its pop. Every operand is declared signed so Quartus maps the multiplies to the embedded multipliers (use-dsp-multipliers): expected 2 × 9 × 9 per vector for the pairs plus one 18 × 18 pair for the MAC, about 18 DSP elements per unit at T_MAX = 8.

The fabric's global pulses map onto the same controls: start = CTRL.start on every unit with ROWS ≠ 0, abort and soft reset = CTRL.abort, clear = CTRL.clear. Verified by lib/sim/tb_matvec_unit.sv: ten cases against a Python-style reference in the testbench (K = 32..1024, T = 1..8, random FIFO gaps, a slow and a blocked lane, saturation, abort and restart, broadcast, ROWS = 0), results bit-exact, one flush per job, all counters checked.

x-buffer addressing: XADDR[15:12] selects the vector t, XADDR[11:0] the byte position in it. The host writes x once per token to the broadcast address (every unit multiplies the same x by its own rows), 4 bytes per DR pass. For stories15M that is 288 bytes per matrix input = 72 passes: a few milliseconds over the direct route. Prefill with T = 8 writes 8 vectors.

Output layout

Lane u receives, row after row, T int32 values: Y_u[(i × T + t)] at word WB_BASE_u + (i × T + t) × 2. The host (ref_matvec.py) reassembles y[row][t] from the N regions and compares with numpy's (Wq.astype(int32) @ x.astype(int32)) grouped and scaled the same way: bit-exact or fail.

Decision asked: T_MAX = 8, int8 × int8 → 48-bit accumulate → int32 with a job-level SHIFT; x written by the host in v1.

The read window: phase P and rd_delay

The chip drives a read word from edge T(CL−1) + tAC (5.4 ns) of its clock and holds it until T(CL) + tOH (2.7 ns). With DRAM_CLK P ns behind clk and CL 3, the word is on DQ from 20 + P + 5.4 to 30 + P + 2.7 ns after the READ edge, plus whatever the board and the FPGA input path add (≈ 5 ns was the fit at 50 MHz in example 06). The FPGA's input register samples at a 10 ns multiple: rd_delay × 10 ns. Simulated against the JEDEC-timed model (tb_sdram_burst_ctrl -D P_NS=… -D RD_DELAY=… -D DQ_DELAY=…):

So the phase and the delay are chosen together and only the board can say which pair it is: the memory test (section 11) sweeps P over builds and rd_delay 2..6 over a register, and the pair with the most margin becomes the default. (The old sdram_model had the data one clock late, which is why example 06's simulation "needed" rd_delay 4 like the board: the new model is JEDEC-exact and the testbenches use 3 for the bare chip.)

Reset: PLL locked through a 2-FF synchronizer → rst_n; the SDRAM init waits its 100 µs after that. KEY0 forces a reset for the memory test. Timing constraints: create_clock 50 MHz on MAX10_CLK1_50, derive_pll_clocks, derive_clock_uncertainty, the SDRAM pins constrained as in example 06 (input/output delays against DRAM_CLK) with the numbers rescaled to 10 ns, and false paths on the TCK crossings as in example 14.

What the last two lines say. With one 16-bit SDRAM the units share 200 MB/s; N does not add bandwidth, it adds independence (each unit starves or stalls on its own, the arbiter keeps the bus full from whoever has room) and places to put compute that later gets its data from somewhere else: the KV cache in M9K, a neighbour unit, a second memory. The lever on throughput today is T (weight reuse), and that is what the prefill demo shows. CYCLES_BUSY / CYCLES_STARVED per unit make this visible on the board.

14 · Out of scope, later versions

  • Softmax, RMSNorm, RoPE, attention, the tokenizer, sampling: the end-to-end LLM is a future project (projects/) that builds on this example; here only the matvec and the data flow.
  • x fetched from the SDRAM X region by the fabric (v2); today the host writes x through jtag_regs.
  • Bank pipelining in the controller (ACT the next burst's bank while the current one streams: overhead → 1–2 clocks, efficiency ≈ 99 % at every length; v2 option when the measurement asks for it).
  • 133 MHz (the -7 part's CL 3 limit is 143 MHz; the PLL gives 50 × 8/3): a stretch, tried only if 100 MHz is stable.
  • KV cache in M9K or in the KV region; unit-to-unit links (the systolic neighbours); several units per channel.
