15 · Stream the SDRAM into compute units

Example 14 fills the 64 MB SDRAM from the PC. This example empties it at the memory's own speed: a 100 MHz burst controller, a fabric of six FIFO channels that share the one memory bus by credits, six compute units that each eat one word per clock (int8 matrix times vector, eight vectors at once), six lanes that carry the results back, and a register file the PC drives over the same JTAG cable. Measured on the board: 196 MB/s over all 64 MB with 0 errors (98 % of the bus's 200 MB/s), 195 MB/s into six units streaming at once, and matrix-vector jobs bit-exact against numpy: random ones up to 512 × 1024 and every matrix of two llama2.c models (stories15M's 15.4 MB of weights in 83.6 ms, one token's worth at 12 tokens a second). It is the data-flow layer of an LLM on this board: the transformer project will stand on it.

Timing: met, worst slack +0.141 ns (all corners, setup, hold, recovery, removal).

Everything that moves data runs on pll clk[0], 100 MHz: the controller, the fabric, the six units, the loader's SDRAM side and the register file's design side. pll clk[1] is the same frequency shifted by 6875 ps and leaves the chip as DRAM_CLK; nothing inside runs on it. clk50 only feeds the PLL and the 7-segment display; the JTAG nodes' cable side runs on the cable's TCK. The six compute units are two thirds of the design (20,205 logic elements, 48 M9Ks, all 120 multiplier elements): each holds its eight activation vectors in M9K and multiplies two weights per clock. The fabric's 18 M9Ks are the six channel FIFOs (1,024 words each) and the six lane FIFOs. Nothing here is only for testing: the counters cost a few hundred registers and are what the measurements below read.

A read burst of L words takes L + 4 clocks (the row open and the CAS latency; the precharge overlaps the next command) and a write burst L + 5 (tWR), so the bus use is about L / (L + 4); the refresh takes the rest (12 clocks every 7.5 µs, 1.6 % of the bus): 98.0 % at 1024. 200 MB/s is the chip: 16 bits at 100 MHz.

The fabric: units streaming at once (fabric_ctl.py bench)

Each unit streams 1,024 rows × 1,024 weights of its own, every unit at the same time, through its channel's FIFO; the units consume one word per clock each, so with two or more they are starved most of the time by design (one bus, N mouths). The rate is the whole stream's words over the longest unit's busy time.

The fabric reaches the memory test's 98 % with two units or more; with one unit the 1,024-word FIFO cannot hold a burst ahead of a consumer that drains as fast as it fills, so 3.6 % is lost to the gap between bursts (the credit rule: a channel asks only when the whole burst fits, so continuity needs DEPTH ≥ 2 × BURST). N adds no bandwidth: it adds independence and places to put compute. The lever on throughput is T, the number of vectors a unit multiplies against each weight it reads.

A job is slower than the bench by the per-row work (a group's scale word, the T results drained at the end of each row) and by its write-back traffic (two words per result, through the lanes, on the same bus). 512 × 1024 × 8 vectors is 4.2 million multiply-accumulates in 2.94 ms: 1.4 GMAC/s from one 16-bit memory. (These ten ran on the build before gotcha 38's setting; on the build on this page 64 × 64 took 2,396 clocks and 512 × 1024 took 293,997, both exact.) The units' own capacity is 9.6 GMAC/s at T = 8 (6 units × 2 weights × 8 vectors per clock); the memory feeds a sixth of it, which is the point of the next step (the KV cache and the small matrices in M9K, a second memory on the GPIO header, or more reuse).

Eight vectors instead of one cost 9 % more time and did eight times the work: 1.32 GMAC/s. One unit at T 1 already keeps the bus 96.8 % busy (a unit eats a word per clock, the memory gives one), so with the classifier the other five units only add independence. At T 8 the units stall instead of starving: eight results per row go back through the lanes, two words each, 512,000 words on top of the 4,896,000 read, on the same bus; the 9 % is exactly that extra traffic, and the lanes waiting behind the channels is where the units spend it. Streaming all 43 matrices once is one token's worth of weights: 83.6 ms at T 1, so this one 16-bit SDRAM bounds a token-at-a-time stories15M at 12 tokens a second, or eight tokens at once in about 90 ms, before the attention and the activations, which never touch the SDRAM. That is the number the Transformer project starts from. The transcript of every matrix: media/jobs_stories15M.txt.

The read window (sdram_memtest.py sweep)

The controller samples the chip's data rd_delay clocks after the READ command. Which clock, and how much margin, depends on the phase of DRAM_CLK: the PLL's dynamic phase port moves it in steps of 208 ps while the memtest runs, 48 steps a period. At the first build's phase 0, rd_delay 4 passed but the compiled phase sat about 300 ps from the edge where the chip starts sampling commands one clock later. The default is now 6875 ps, the centre of the rd_delay-4 window: it passes from −16 to +16 steps (±3.3 ns); rd_delay 3 passes one command clock earlier (+19..+27); 5 never. The whole map, both builds: media/memtest_sweep.txt.

Block diagram

Two virtual JTAG nodes share the cable. Node 0 is example 14's loader, unchanged, on the fabric's single-word port: it fills the memory with a model and reads results back. Node 1 is jtag_regs, a generic register bus (lib/rtl/jtag_regs.sv, scripts/jtag_regs.py): ADDR, WRITE with auto-increment, READ, and an ID; every block answers its own addresses and 0 elsewhere, so the top ORs the read data. One round trip is about 2 ms, so the PC batches a whole job's setup (dozens of registers, the activation vectors) into one scan.

How a job flows

  1. The weights are in the SDRAM. fabric_ctl.py load (or job --load) streams the file through the loader: a row is K int8 weights in groups of 32, each group followed by its int16 scale (17 words a group); rows start on a word boundary.
  2. The PC programs one job in one scan. For each unit u: ROWS, K, T, SHIFT; channel u: BASE (the first word of unit u's slice of rows), LEN, BURST; lane u: WB_BASE (where its results go). The T activation vectors (int8, K each) are written once to the broadcast address 0x1F0: every unit's x memory takes them. CLEAR resets the counters; START starts all units at once.
  3. The channels fill their FIFOs. Each channel asks the arbiter for a burst whenever its FIFO has room for the whole burst (a credit); the arbiter grants round-robin, the single-word port first when the loader wants in; the controller opens the row, reads BURST words at one per clock, precharges. A refresh pauses a burst and the burst resumes.
  4. The units eat. A unit pops a word per clock when its FIFO shows one (show-ahead, no request latency): two int8 weights, multiplied against the same two positions of all T vectors (2 × T MACs a clock), summed in 48 bits per group, scaled by the group's int16 scale when it arrives, accumulated per row. At the end of a row the T results are shifted right by SHIFT, saturated to int32, and pushed into the unit's lane.
  5. The lanes write back. A lane turns 32-bit results into pairs of 16-bit words and, when it holds WBURST of them (or the unit flushes at its end), asks the arbiter for a write burst to its pointer and advances it.
  6. The PC waits for idle (STATUS bit 6: no channel running, no unit busy, no lane holding data), reads the counters first (the window closes at that read, gotcha 39), then reads the results back through the loader and compares every int32 with ref_matvec.py: the same arithmetic in numpy (int64 sums, the same shift and saturation).

Things learned (each cost real time)

  1. Credits, not backpressure (spec, decision 2). The controller never waits for a consumer: a channel asks for a burst only when the whole burst fits, so a granted burst always lands. The bus stays at 98 % with six consumers of any speed, and the controller needs no rready. Continuity for one consumer needs DEPTH ≥ 2 × BURST.
  2. An arbiter failed timing by 8 ns at 100 MHz (gotcha 35): a credit subtraction, a six-way round robin and a grant in one clock. Registered in three steps; the grant queue hides the pipeline from the controller.
  3. Closing 100 MHz on the MAX 10 is a list of six shapes, not a mystery (gotcha 37): registered credits and round-robin, M9K outputs registered, a register after every DSP multiply, a register before every dynamic array select, one-hot selects, a head register on a show-ahead FIFO that feeds a bus. −8.4 ns to +0.4 ns in three builds, by grouping report_timing -npaths 200 by shape instead of chasing paths.
  4. Six synchronizers became one M9K (gotcha 38): Quartus' Auto Shift Register Replacement turns any chain of registers into an altshift_taps block and merges equal ones across instances. The sign: a module with a few bits of "memory" and no RAM. AUTO_SHIFT_REGISTER_RECOGNITION OFF is in every generated .qsf now.
  5. The read window has two edges and wraps around (gotcha 40): a sweep over a whole period must be read circularly; the first build's phase was 300 ps from an edge and still passed. Centre it (6875 ps), then measure the margin (±3.3 ns). The JEDEC-exact model wants rd_delay 3 for the bare chip; the board, with its pins and input registers, wants 4. The testbenches set 3, the RTL defaults to 4.
  6. The counter window is not the job (gotcha 39): BUSY / TOTAL / REFRESHES run from CLEAR until the PC reads them, and a JTAG round trip is 2 ms, so on a 23 µs job the bus looks 105 % busy. Read them first after the idle poll and compute rates from the units' own busy counters.
  7. A simulation deadlocked with X (gotcha 36): an always @(*) block whose inputs are all constants never runs in Icarus, so its output stays X forever. always_comb runs once at time 0.
  8. The refresh is 1.6 % of the bus (12 clocks every 7.5 µs) and pauses bursts instead of waiting for them: a 1,024-word burst is longer than the refresh interval, so forbidding refresh inside bursts would cost more than pausing.

Full detail: gotchas 35–40; the design decisions and the review's questions: spec, sections 13 and 17.

Use it in your own design

python scripts/new_project.py my_designs/x x_top --lib dps fabric matvec jtagregs usbloader and src/extra.qsf from here for the SDRAM pins. Wire sdram_burst_ctrl to the pins, stream_fabric between it and your consumers (ch_dout / ch_valid / ch_rd_en in, wb_data / wb_valid / wb_ready / wb_flush back), jtag_regs for the PC and sdram_loader on the single-word port: src/fabric_top.sv is the wiring, the stream-from-sdram skill the short form. Your consumer can be anything that pops a word when it wants one; matvec_unit is one.
