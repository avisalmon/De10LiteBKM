13 · Keep a design after power-off

Every other example loads a .sof: it runs until the power goes off. The MAX 10 also has its own configuration flash: write a design there and the board starts it by itself at every power-up.

Flashed and verified on the board 2026-10-24 Power-cycle check: to do by hand Skill: flash-design
Download gamepad.pof (for the flash) Its project: gamepad.qar (example 04)

Every build in this BKM already makes both: output_files/<name>.sof and <name>.pof (the projects use INTERNAL_FLASH_UPDATE_MODE "SINGLE IMAGE WITH ERAM", so ROM and RAM contents are in the flash image too).

Do it

python scripts/de10.py flash examples/04_gamepad       # writes and verifies the flash
→ Info: Quartus Prime Programmer was successful. 0 errors, 0 warnings
→ OK: flashed and verified; it starts at the next power-up

Under the hood: quartus_pgm -m jtag -o "pv;output_files/gamepad.pof" (p = program, v = verify). Or in the Quartus Programmer: add the .pof, tick Program/Configure and Verify on the CFM and UFM rows, Start.

Measured on the board

  • The game-pad tester (example 04) was flashed and verified without errors.
  • Right after programming, the MAX 10 reloaded itself from the new flash image: the board answered over USB with example 04's status line, its frame counter showing it had just started.
  • Still to do by hand: unplug the USB cable, plug it back, and see the game-pad screen come up without the PC doing anything. (This replaces whatever the board started with before; the original streaks demo is gone.)

Good to know

  • Loading a .sof afterwards does not touch the flash: develop as usual, and the flashed design comes back at the next power-up.
  • The MAX 10 can also hold two images and choose at power-up (the dual-image mode, selected by the CONFIG_SEL pin, or switched from your design with the Dual Configuration IP). Not built here.
  • The UFM (user flash memory) part of the flash can hold your own data that survives power-off, through the On-Chip Flash IP. Not built here.
