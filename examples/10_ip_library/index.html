10 · Using IP from Altera's library

Square root, division and a dual-clock FIFO, straight from Altera's library: no wizard, no generated files, no license. Put to work measuring gravity with the on-board accelerometer.

The three megafunctions

altsqrt #(.width(32), .q_port_width(16), .r_port_width(17), .pipeline(4), .lpm_type("altsqrt")) sqrt (
  .radical(sumsq), .clk(clk), .ena(1'b1), .aclr(1'b0), .q(mag), .remainder());

lpm_divide #(.lpm_widthn(24), .lpm_widthd(16), .lpm_nrepresentation("SIGNED"), .lpm_drepresentation("UNSIGNED"),
  .lpm_pipeline(12), .lpm_type("LPM_DIVIDE")) div (
  .numer(z100), .denom(mag), .clock(clk), .clken(1'b1), .aclr(1'b0), .quotient(quot), .remain(rem));

dcfifo #(.lpm_width(32), .lpm_widthu(4), .lpm_numwords(16), .lpm_showahead("OFF"), .lpm_type("dcfifo"),
  .rdsync_delaypipe(4), .wrsync_delaypipe(4), .intended_device_family("MAX 10"), ...) fifo (
  .data(wdata), .wrclk(clk50), .wrreq(wrreq), .wrfull(wrfull), .rdclk(clk25), .rdreq(rdreq), .q(rdata), .rdempty(rdempty), ...);

Where to find the ports and parameters of any megafunction: C:\intelFPGA_lite\17.1\quartus\eda\sim_lib\altera_mf.v (altsqrt, dcfifo, altsyncram ...) and 220model.v (the lpm_* ones). The same files let ModelSim simulate them.

Pipelines: latency for speed

The divider first had 4 pipeline stages and failed timing at 50 MHz by 1.9 ns. With 12 stages it passes: the answer comes 12 clocks later instead of 4, which costs nothing here (a new sample arrives every 10 ms). That trade, more stages for a higher clock, is the knob almost every arithmetic IP gives you. Count the latency and wait for it (the design waits 14 clocks after the square root).

dcfifo vs our byte_cdc

dcfifo is Altera's dual-clock FIFO: any width and depth, in block RAM, with full / empty / used-words flags on both sides. lib/rtl/byte_cdc.sv (example 04) does the same job for bytes in plain Verilog. Use dcfifo when you want wide words or deep buffers.
