Requirements

  • The arcade Pac-Man's maze, dots, power pellets, four ghosts with the arcade's targeting and mode timing, frightened mode, eyes returning home, lives, levels with rising speed, a high score, the intro tune and effects.
  • Runs on the DE10-Lite with the LCD / game-pad shield, from the library's display chain (VGA pins and LCD together).
  • Playable by a person with the joystick and Start, and by the AI over USB (letters), so it can be tested without hands.
  • The screen can be read back over USB (debug_link) for automatic checks.
  • Fits comfortably in the MAX 10 10M50 with the debug capture buffer: under 25 % logic, under 70 % memory.
  • Fully testable in simulation: a testbench renders frames and plays the game.

Clock domains and crossings

DomainWhat runs thereCrossing
50 MHz (pll c1)ADC IP, gamepad, debounce→ 25 MHz: two flip-flops per button in pacman_top. Buttons are slow levels, so no handshake is needed; a one-frame delay is invisible.
25 MHz (pll c0)VGA timing, pac_logic, pac_draw, pac_sound, debug_link, jtag_uart, 7-segmentnone inside: the screen reads the game's registers directly. They change only during vertical blank (the sequencer runs right after tick), so a frame is always drawn from one consistent state.
100 MHz (pll c2)lcd_ctrl inside Screens_dispalythe library's own; it samples the 25 MHz rgb and syncs.

The JTAG UART's rx_valid is a 25 MHz pulse. The game's letters are decoded in pacman_top; 's' is held in a register until the next tick so the game, which samples start only at ticks, cannot miss it.

Coordinate system and screen geometry

Scan640 × 480 at 25 MHz from vga_controller: pxl_x, pxl_y (32-bit, as the library defines them)
Art grid320 × 240: lx = pxl_x[9:1], ly = pxl_y[8:1]; every art pixel is 2 × 2 scan pixels. The USB capture (STEP 2) is exactly this grid.
Maze28 × 31 tiles of 8 = 224 × 248 maze pixels, at art columns 48..271. mx = lx - 48, my = ly + 4: 4 rows of the outer wall are cut at the top and the bottom (the maze is 8 rows taller than the screen).
Tilestile (c, r) = (x >> 3, y >> 3); the centre of a tile is x[2:0] = 4, y[2:0] = 4. Decisions happen only at centres.
Directions0 up, 1 left, 2 down, 3 right (the arcade's tie order); reverse = d ^ 2
Fixed pointsPac-Man start (112, 188); the house door (112, 92); inside the house y = 116, Inky at x 96, Clyde at x 128; the tunnel is row 14, open past both edges, x wraps 0 ↔ 223
Side panelsleft: art columns 0..47 (6 text cells): 1UP score, HIGH, LEVEL; right: 272..319: LIVES, spare-life icons, PAC MAN

Both big memories answer one clock after the address. The screen therefore computes the address from the next scan pixel (pxl_x + 1) and uses the answer for the current one; the wall lookup and the dot lookup both work this way, so no pixel is shifted. The testbench primes each line by starting at pxl_x = -1.

pac_logic: state, sequencer, encodings

Per-actor registers

Five actors, index 0 Pac-Man, 1 Blinky, 2 Pinky, 3 Inky, 4 Clyde: ax, ay (8-bit maze pixels), ad (2-bit direction), acc (5-bit speed accumulator, sixteenths), gst (3-bit ghost state), gfr (frightened).

The micro-sequencer (ms)

Started by a tick in G_PLAY. One actor (a) at a time; the actor's registers are copied into working registers cx, cy, cd, cst and written back in M_DONE after every pixel step.

StateClocksDoes
M_ACTOR1load the actor; acc += speed; steps = acc >> 4 (0..2). Pac-Man skips the frame after eating a dot (pac_pause).
M_STEP1Pac-Man: a reverse request is taken at once; at a centre ask about the wanted tile → M_PWANT, else M_MOVE. Ghost: by cst: HOUSE waits; LEAVE / ENTER steer by coordinates; ACTIVE / EYES at a centre → M_TGT (EYES at a door tile → ENTER), else M_MOVE.
M_PWANT, M_PFWD2wanted tile open → take it; then ask about the tile ahead: wall → stop (M_DONE), else M_MOVE
M_TGT1choose the target tile (EYES: the door; scatter: the corner; chase: the ghost's rule); start k at 0, or at a random direction when frightened
M_GQ, M_GR2 × up to 4ask about direction k; frightened: the first open non-reverse way wins; else keep the open way with the smallest squared distance to the target (ties: the lower k). After 4 tries take the best (or reverse if all shut, which this maze never does).
M_MOVE1one pixel along cd, wrapping in the tunnel; Pac-Man → M_EAT, a ghost → M_DONE
M_EAT..M_EAT33at a centre read the dot RAM under Pac-Man (2 clocks), then clear it, add 10 / 50 in BCD, count, set pac_pause; a power pellet starts fr_timer, frightens ACTIVE / HOUSE / LEAVE ghosts and reverses the active ones
M_DONE1write back; another step or M_NEXT
M_NEXT1next actor, or the collision pass
M_COLL4ghost cg vs Pac-Man: ACTIVE and |dx| ≤ 4 and |dy| ≤ 4: frightened → EYES, points 200 · 2^chain, G_FREEZE; else G_DYING unless invincible. Only in G_PLAY, so a second ghost on the same frame waits for the freeze to end.
M_TIMERS1frightened countdown (all gfr cleared at 1); scatter/chase mode steps (420, 1200, 420, 1200, 300, 1200, 300 frames, then chase for ever; each change reverses ACTIVE ghosts; paused while frightened); house releases; last dot → G_LEVEL

Longest frame: 5 actors × 2 steps × about 12 clocks + 4 + 1 ≈ 130 clocks. Vertical blank is 36,000.

pac_draw: the pixel pipeline

Purely combinational from (pxl_x, pxl_y) and the game's registers, plus the two memories addressed one pixel ahead. For every pixel, in priority order:

  1. Ghosts (Blinky in front): ghost_sprite gives hit and a colour. The body is a 14-row bitmap with two skirt frames (frame[3]); eyes are two 4 × 5 whites shifted a pixel toward the direction with 2 × 2 pupils; frightened: blue body, a wavy mouth, white/red while flashing; EYES / ENTER: only the eyes.
  2. Pac-Man: a 13-pixel disc on half-pixel centres (dx2 = 2(mx - x) + 1, dx2² + dy2² ≤ 169) minus a wedge: the pixel is in the mouth when a·sin(t) > |b|·cos(t), with (a, b) the pixel rotated into the facing direction and t the half-opening (0°, 25°, 45° by pac_mouth; 15°..180° by death_phase while dying). Products go through 12-bit wires: the compare would otherwise be done at 6 bits.
  3. Text: one font8x8; the character comes from the panel tables (screen grid) or the maze messages (READY!, GAME OVER, PUSH START on tile row 17). Numbers are BCD digits with leading zeros blanked, the last two always shown.
  4. Spare-life icons: small left-facing Pac-Men, one per spare life.
  5. Dots (2 × 2 at the tile centre) and power pellets (a 7-pixel disc, blinking) from rb_q.
  6. Walls from the ROM: blue, white while maze_white; the door pink.

pac_sound

A square wave: a 16-bit counter toggles the output when it reaches half (clocks per half period at 25 MHz, half = 25e6 / (2f)). half is chosen once per frame from the highest-priority effect still running: death (96 frames, falling with a warble), the intro tune (31 notes, {half, frames} table, 256 frames), ghost eaten (30 frames, rising), power pellet (40 frames, two low notes), dot (6 frames, two notes whose order alternates: "waka"). 'm' mutes. The output drives ARDUINO_IO[12], the shield's buzzer line.

Design decisions

  • No frame buffer for the game. The picture is computed per pixel from a few hundred bits of state; the only memories are the static wall picture and the 2-bit dot map. Cheap, and nothing can tear.
  • Sequential game update. One actor and one pixel per few clocks instead of parallel per-actor logic: a fifth of the logic, one tile-table port, and a state machine you can single-step in a waveform.
  • BCD everywhere a number is shown. Score, high score and level never need a divider; the screen and the 7-segment read the same digits.
  • Tables generated by Python. The maze is a 31-line text picture in make_maze.py; it writes the Verilog table, the wall picture (with the arcade's inset double lines computed from distance to open tiles) and a preview PNG. Edit the text, run the script, rebuild.
  • Letters over USB. Five lines of decode in the top make the whole game testable by a machine.
  • Kept out, on purpose: the fruit, the pop-up score, the demo in attract mode. They are easy additions and are listed on the overview page.
