Your first project in Quartus

Today you do everything yourself, by hand, in the Quartus window: create a project, choose the chip, connect pins, compile, and load it into the board. The result is small: a switch that turns on an LED. The skill is big.

Why by hand?

From lesson 3 on, the chat does these steps for you with one command each. After today you'll know exactly what those commands do, and you could do it all without the chat.

About 45 minutes. The chat opens Quartus for you and checks your work.

Lesson 2 · the project

A Quartus project is a folder with a few files

  • .qpf: the project file. Quartus opens this.
  • .qsf: the settings: which chip, which files, which pin each signal uses. Plain text: you can read it in VS Code.
  • .sv: your design, in SystemVerilog.
  • .sof: the result of compiling, the file you load into the chip.
The chat has already put first_led.sv into your folder: my_designs\lessons\02_first_project\first_led. It tells you the full path.

Lesson 2 · step 2 · the wizard

Tell Quartus which chip

Quartus builds for one exact chip, and there are hundreds. Ours is printed on the big black chip on the board:

10M50DAF484C7G

  1. Family: MAX 10 (already chosen).
  2. Type 10M50DAF484C7G into Name filter.
  3. Click the one line left in the list. Click Next.

The name tells a lot: 10M = MAX 10, 50 = about 50 thousand logic elements, F484 = 484 pins, C7 = speed grade.

EDA Tool Settings: leave everything at <None>. Click Next.

Summary: check the folder, the name and the device. Click Finish.

Lesson 2 · your design

Read your design: five lines

module first_led (
	input		sw0,
	output	led0
	);

assign led0 = sw0;

endmodule
  • module first_led: a box called first_led. That's your whole circuit.
  • input sw0, output led0: the box's two wires to the outside world. One comes in, one goes out. The names are ours: they don't mean anything to the chip yet.
  • assign led0 = sw0;: connect them. Whatever the switch says, the LED shows.
  • endmodule: the end of the box.
You'll learn to write Verilog properly in lesson 4. Today, just read it. Open first_led.sv in VS Code and find each line.
Lesson 2 · step 3

Let Quartus read your design

Menu Processing > Start > Start Analysis & Synthesis (or Ctrl+K).

Quartus reads the Verilog, checks it, and finds the inputs and outputs. A few seconds later: successful.

Look: 0 logic elements!A wire from a switch to an LED needs no logic at all. Quartus noticed, and used none. It found 2 pins: sw0 and led0.
Lesson 2 · the new idea

Pins: which leg of the chip is which

The chip has 484 little balls underneath (that's the "484" in its name). Each one is a pin.

When Terasic made the board, they wired switch SW0 to pin C10, and LED LEDR0 to pin A8. That's copper on the board: it can't change.

Quartus doesn't know how the board is wired. So you tell it: sw0 is on pin C10, led0 is on pin A8.

And the voltageEvery pin also needs its electrical standard. The DE10-Lite's switches and LEDs use 3.3-V LVTTL.
Lesson 2 · step 4

The Pin Planner

Menu Assignments > Pin Planner. At the bottom is a table with your two signals.

  1. Location of led0: double-click the cell, type A8, Enter. For sw0: C10.
  2. I/O Standard: it says 2.5 V (default), which is wrong for our board. Double-click, open the list, choose 3.3-V LVTTL. Typing 3 jumps near it.
  3. Two red dots appear on the chip map: your pins. Close the Pin Planner; it saves by itself.
Lesson 2 · step 5

Ask the chat to check your project

Before compiling, tell the chat: "check my project". It reads your .qsf (the settings file) and checks every setting you made. Something wrong? It tells you which setting and where to fix it in Quartus.

OK device 10M50DAF484C7G OK top-level entity first_led OK first_led.sv is in the project OK sw0 on PIN_C10 OK sw0 I/O standard 3.3-V LVTTL OK led0 on PIN_A8 OK led0 I/O standard 3.3-V LVTTL Ready to compile.

Curious? Open first_led.qsf in VS Code. Your clicks became plain lines of text:

set_location_assignment PIN_A8 -to led0
set_location_assignment PIN_C10 -to sw0
set_instance_assignment -name IO_STANDARD
    "3.3-V LVTTL" -to led0
Lesson 2 · step 6

Compile

Menu Processing > Start Compilation (or Ctrl+L). About 30 seconds. The Tasks panel ticks off each step from lesson 1: synthesis, fitter, assembler, timing.

Success looks like"Full Compilation was successful. 0 errors".
Warnings and a red line?Warnings are normal. The red TimeQuest line means there is no timing file yet: fine, this design has no clock. Errors are the ones that matter, and the chat helps you read them.
Lesson 2 · step 7

Load it into the chip

  1. Menu Tools > Programmer. Your file output_files/first_led.sof is already in the list.
  2. It says No Hardware? Click Hardware Setup, double-click USB-Blaster, click Close.
  3. Click Start. A few seconds: 100% (Successful).
Lesson 2 · it works

Flip SW0

Flip switch SW0 up: LED0 lights. Down: it goes out. That's your design, running in the chip.

The other LEDs glow a little?

Normal. Pins your design doesn't use get a weak pull-up inside the chip, enough to make an LED glow faintly. Only LED0 follows your switch.

The number digits are dark?

Same reason: their segments light up on 0, and the weak pull-up gives them a 1.

The screen shows an old picture?

The LCD keeps the last picture it was sent. Your design doesn't use the screen at all.

Lesson 2 · your change

Make it yours: SW9 drives LED9 too

Now the whole flow once more, on your own:

  1. In VS Code, add to first_led.sv an input sw9, an output led9, and a line that connects them. (Look at how sw0 and led0 are written, and follow the pattern.) Save.
  2. In Quartus: Ctrl+K so it sees the new names.
  3. Pin Planner: sw9 on F15, led9 on B11, both 3.3-V LVTTL.
  4. Ask the chat to check, then compile (Ctrl+L) and program.
  5. Flip SW9.
Stuck?Ask the chat for a hint. It won't write the line for you, but it will help you find it. A compile error is not a failure: read the first red line, it usually says exactly which line of your file is wrong.

How does it know the pins?

The course keeps a list of every pin on the board (lib/board/de10_lite_pins.csv), using the board's own names: SW[0] is C10, LEDR[0] is A8, and so on. If your ports use those names, the pins are filled in for you. That's why later lessons call their inputs SW and their outputs LEDR.
